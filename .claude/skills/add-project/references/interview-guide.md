# Interview Guide

Each section lists **what you need to come away with**, then starting questions. The questions are prompts, not a script: adapt them to what the Daily Log already shows. The sections map to the case study structure in `case-study-structure.md`. The right-hand label shows which Quarry field each section fills.

---

## 1. Stakes → Business Context

**Need:** why it existed, who asked, what it was costing the business, and why it mattered now.

- Who brought this to you, or did you find it yourself? What exactly did they ask for?
- What was it costing before you got involved: money, hours, risk, frustration? How do you know?
- Why hadn't anyone fixed it already?

## 2. Scope → Business Context / Source Evidence

**Need:** duration, team size, the most senior person involved, and the environment. Recruiters judge your level from this.

- How long, from first contact to the outcome you'd point to?
- Who was on it with you, and in what roles?
- Who was the most senior person who saw it or decided something about it?

## 3. Ownership → Stakeholders (+ People rows)

**Need:** a clear split between *your decisions* and *other people's contributions*, plus each important stakeholder's stake.

- Name the decisions that were yours alone. What would have happened without you?
- Who else made it work, and what exactly did they do?
- For each key stakeholder: what do they care about or get measured on, what did they decide, and where could this relationship lead?

## 4. Turning point → Discovery Notes

**Need:** one specific decision, the alternatives considered, and why you chose it. Best case: you set aside your own earlier idea.

- What was the moment the project could have gone a different way?
- What were the other options? Why did you reject them?
- What did you believe at the start that turned out to be wrong?
- What did someone else miss that you saw? How did you see it?

## 5. Outcomes → Outcomes

**Need:** business, organizational, technical, and learning outcomes, **each labeled realized / projected / validated-by-whom**.

- What has actually happened, measured? What's still a projection?
- Who validated the number (finance, ops, a VP)? How did it change between first estimate and final?
- What changed in how people work, decide, or talk about this?
- What artifact exists: email, deck, BRD, dashboard, quote?

## 6. Setback → Reflection

**Need:** a real misstep **you own**, what you did about it, and what you'd do differently.

- What didn't go the way you planned?
- What was your part in that? Leave others' parts aside for now.
- What did you change afterward, and what happened?
- If you started over tomorrow, what would you do differently?

## 7. What's still open → Outcomes / Reflection

**Need:** what isn't proven yet, and what would prove it.

- What's not done or not proven?
- What evidence would change your mind, or confirm it?
- What's the next decision, and who makes it?

## 8. What it proves → Reflection

**Need:** the one capability this proves, and how it fits the Career Thesis.

- If a recruiter remembered one thing about you from this, what should it be?
- Does this support the Career Thesis, push against it, or add something new?

## Red flags: probe when you hear these

| You hear | Probe with |
|---|---|
| **"We"** with no "I" | "Which part of that was your call specifically?" |
| **A number with no source** | "Where does that figure come from? Who'd back it up?" |
| **"Projected" with no validator** | "Who reviewed that projection besides you?" |
| **Blame** ("eng wouldn't engage") | "What was within your control there? What did you try?" |
| **Humblebrag** ("I cared too much") | "Give me a moment where you actually got it wrong." |
| **An outcome with no decision** | "What did you decide that led to that?" |
| **Big organizational claims** ("changed how the org thinks about AI") | "What's the evidence? Who said it, or what changed?" |
| **A claim in LinkedIn or the Career Thesis that isn't in the Daily Log** | "I can't find this in the log. What's the source?" |

## Pacing

- If an answer is rich, follow it before moving to the next section.
- If he says "I don't know" or "I'd have to check," write it down as an open item with a named source to check. Don't guess.
- Close each section by saying what you captured in two lines, so mistakes surface early.
